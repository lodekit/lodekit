---
title: The dashboard
description: "Tour the Lodekit dashboard: browsing apps, services, and settings from your browser."
---

The dashboard is Lodekit's control room — a web page at
`http://lodekit.localhost:7777` where you see your apps, watch what they're
doing, and tune how everything behaves. It's a regular browser tab: open it
from the menubar, bookmark it, keep it pinned.

Across the top you'll find the Lodekit logo (a click takes you home), a
services menu, and the Settings gear. Press <kbd>⌘K</kbd> anywhere to open the
command palette and jump straight to any app, service view, or setting by
typing its name.

## Home

**Home** shows one card per app: its icon, name, and description. The cards
are live — when your agent saves a change, the card shows *building…*, then
flips to *ready* (or *error*, with the problem spelled out). If an app still
needs credentials, its card shows an "N secrets needed" badge so you know
before you open it. With no apps installed yet, Home simply suggests asking
your agent to build one.

## Data

**Data** is the explorer for everything your apps have stored. Pick an app and
you get an overview — how many rows, keys, files, and events it holds, small
charts of where the space goes, and a feed of what was added recently. From
there, browse each store directly: any table (with filtering, multi-column
sorting, search, and paging), the key-value entries, uploaded files with their
metadata, and the app's own events. Everything is read-only — looking never
changes your data — and a refresh button picks up whatever your apps wrote in
the meantime. A per-app storage breakdown is coming soon.

## Logs

**Logs** is a live feed of what Lodekit and your apps are reporting — new
lines appear as they happen. Filter by severity (debug, info, warning, error)
or by app, and expand any row for its full details. When something misbehaves,
this is the first place to look — and your agent can read the same records to
debug for you. Traffic through your gateways lands here too — every request
gets a line, with credentials always masked — and a source filter shows just
one provider's traffic.

## Events

**Events** shows the platform's announcement stream: apps becoming ready,
records changing, tasks finishing. Like Logs it follows along live, but it
also keeps history, so you can scroll back and filter by app, type, or text to
see what happened while you were away.

## Secrets

**Secrets** is where your credentials live — the API keys and tokens your
apps need. You add a secret once, then link it to any app that asks for one.
Values are write-only: you can paste a new value, replace it, or delete the
secret, but the dashboard never shows a value back — not to you, not to your
agent.

## Gateways

**Gateways** shows the outside providers your apps can reach — one card per
provider, with its icon and how many apps use it. Open a gateway to finish an
app's connection: link a secret into each slot the provider needs, fill in
any config values, or click **Connect** for providers that use OAuth sign-in.
Each connection wears a status badge — connected, needs setup, needs reauth,
unhealthy, or error — and every gateway has its own Logs link, so you can see
exactly what's been asked of that provider.

## Settings

**Settings** collects every knob in one place: **General** and **Appearance**
for the platform, **Services** for how storage, tasks, and the rest behave,
and one section per app for the knobs its builder declared. Changed values
are marked as modified and can be reset to their defaults with one click; the
rarely-needed ones sit in a collapsed *Advanced* group. Each app's section
can also *override* a service setting just for that app — revert the override
and the shared value applies again.

:::note
**Tasks** and **Themes** appear in the services menu but are coming soon.
Theming works today [through a CSS file](/guide/theming/).
:::

Everything the dashboard shows lives in one folder on your Mac — see
[Your Lodekit folder](/guide/your-lodekit-folder/) for what's where and how
to back it up.
