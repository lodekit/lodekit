---
title: The menubar manager
description: "What the menubar manager does day to day: starting, stopping, and supervising the runtime."
---

The manager is the Lodekit icon in your menu bar — the only piece of Lodekit
that looks like a Mac app. It has one job: keep the platform running. It
starts Lodekit when you launch it, watches over it while you work, and gives
you the on/off switches.

It's also self-contained. `Lodekit.app` carries everything it needs to run
the platform, including its own private copy of Node — it installs nothing
system-wide and touches nothing else on your Mac. Everything
you actually *use* is the [dashboard](/guide/dashboard/) in your browser; the
menubar is just the supervisor.

## The tray menu

Click the menubar icon and you get:

- **Status line** — what Lodekit is doing right now: *starting* while it
  boots, then *running*. You'll see *stopped* after you stop it yourself, or
  *error* if something went wrong.
- **Dashboard** — opens the dashboard in your browser.
- **Settings** — opens the dashboard straight to its Settings page.
- **Launch at Login** — start Lodekit automatically when you log in to your
  Mac. This toggle and the one in the dashboard's Settings → General are the
  same switch: change either and the other follows. If you change it in
  macOS System Settings instead, the system's choice wins and Lodekit adopts
  it.
- **Start / Stop / Restart** — the power switches. Stop shuts the platform
  down; Start brings it back; Restart does both in one go.
- **Quit Lodekit** — quits the manager and stops the platform with it.

## If something goes wrong

Lodekit never restarts itself behind your back. If the platform crashes, the
status line switches to *error* and stays there until you choose **Restart** —
so a problem is always visible, never silently looping. Before restarting,
it's worth a glance at the [Logs view](/guide/dashboard/#logs) (or ask your
agent to look) to see what happened.

## Quitting

Quitting the manager stops everything: the dashboard and your apps are
unavailable until you launch `Lodekit.app` again. Nothing is lost — your apps
and data sit safely in [your Lodekit folder](/guide/your-lodekit-folder/) —
they're just not being served. If you'd rather never think about it, turn on
**Launch at Login** and Lodekit is simply always there.

One more thing you can shape from outside an app's code: how everything
looks. That's [Theming](/guide/theming/).
